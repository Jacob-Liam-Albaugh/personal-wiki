# Universal property

**Summary**: A characterization of an object by a unique-factorization condition, which determines it up to unique isomorphism; products, coproducts, limits and colimits are the standard cases.

---

## Definition pattern

A universal property singles out an object by a unique factorization; two such objects are isomorphic (Prop. 3.84: terminal objects are isomorphic; Remark 3.85: "the" versus "a" limit; Remark 6.9 likewise for colimits).

## Limits

- A terminal object has a unique morphism from every object (Def. 3.79, p. 108).
- A product $X\times Y$ has projections such that every pair $T\to X$, $T\to Y$ factors uniquely through it (Def. 3.86). Products in Set are Cartesian products (Ex. 3.87); products of categories (Ex. 3.89).
- A cone over a diagram $D:J\to C$; a limit is a universal cone (Def. 3.92, p. 111). Terminal objects and products are limits over the empty and discrete graphs (Exs. 3.93-3.94).
- Limits in Set are computed as compatible tuples (Thm. 3.95, p. 112). Pullbacks come from a cospan shape (Ex. 3.99); the pullback is not the instance pullback $\Delta_F$ of [[Functor]] (Remark 3.100).

## Colimits

> [!definition] Initial object, coproduct, pushout
> An initial object has a unique morphism to every object. A coproduct $A+B$ has injections and a unique copairing $[f,g]$ out of it. A pushout $X+_A Y$ of $f:A\to X$, $g:A\to Y$ is the universal cocone over the span. (source: Seven Sketches, Defs. 6.1, 6.11, 6.19, pp. 185-188)

- Colimits are limits in $C^{op}$ (Def. 3.102, p. 114, deferred to ch. 6 for details).
- Initial objects in preorders are bottom elements (Ex. 6.2); coproducts in a preorder are joins (Exercise 6.13); coproducts in Set and FinSet are disjoint unions (Ex. 6.14).
- Pushouts in a preorder, of isomorphic spans, and in FinSet as a quotient of a disjoint union (Exs. 6.22-6.29).
- Finite colimits (Def. 6.30). $C$ has all finite colimits iff it has an initial object and all pushouts (Prop. 6.32). Set and FinSet have them (Cor. 6.36); the formula is a quotient of the coproduct of the $D(v)$ by the relation generated by the arrows (Thm. 6.37). Coequalizers are a special case (Exs. 6.38-6.42).
- Pushouts are how cospans compose, hence how circuits are glued: [[Hypergraph category]].

## Free structures

Prop. 5.29 (p. 156): prop functors $\mathrm{Free}(G)\to\mathcal C$ correspond one-to-one with assignments of each generator $g$ to a morphism $s(g)\to t(g)$ in $\mathcal C$; a presented prop $\langle G\mid E\rangle$ also has a universal property (Remark 5.34): [[Signal flow graph]]. Free preorder on a relation (Ex. 5.19), free category on a graph (Ex. 5.22, [[Category]]).

## Examples elsewhere

- Products and limits in Poly: [[Poly product]].
- Cofree comonoid: [[Polynomial comonoid]].
- Subobject classifier, the universal property behind logic in a topos (Def. 7.12): [[Topos]].

Wiki note: a universal property that holds for all inputs assembles into an [[Adjunction]].
