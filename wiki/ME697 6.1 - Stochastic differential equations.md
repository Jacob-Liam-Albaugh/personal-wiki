## Key results

**Donsker construction** (source: 02_bm.ipynb). A random walk $S_n=\sum_{i\le n}X_i$ with $E[X_i]=0$, $V[X_i]=1$, rescaled diffusively,

$$
W(t) = S_{nt}/\sqrt{n},
$$

converges to Brownian motion. Simulation: $W(t+\Delta t)=W(t)+\sqrt{\Delta t}\,Z$. Checked with 1000 samples: $E[W(t)]=0$, $V[W(t)]=t$.

**Brownian bridge.**

$$
B(t) = W(t) - \tfrac{t}{T}\,W(T),
\qquad V[B(t)] = t(1-t)\ \ (T=1).
$$

Its KL expansion uses eigenfunctions of the Dirichlet Poisson problem:

$$
B(t) = \sum_{n\ge1} Z_n\,\frac{\sqrt2\,\sin(n\pi t)}{n\pi}.
$$

With 100 terms paths look rough; 1000 terms and 1000 samples recover the variance.

**2D version.** The book's "Brownian sheet" ("basically a 2D Brownian bridge"):

$$
B(t_1,t_2) = \sum_{n,m} Z_{nm}\,\frac{2\sin(n\pi t_1)\sin(m\pi t_2)}{nm\,\pi^2}.
$$

**Geometric Brownian motion** (source: 03_stochastic_exponential_growth.ipynb):

$$
dX = \mu X\,dt + \sigma X\,dW.
$$

Itô: $Y=\ln X$ gives $dY=(\mu-\sigma^2/2)\,dt+\sigma\,dW$. With $\theta=\mu-\sigma^2/2$ and increments $\Delta Y_i\sim N(\theta\Delta t_i,\ \sigma^2\Delta t_i)$ the MLEs are closed form:

$$
\hat\theta = \frac{\sum_i \Delta Y_i}{\sum_i \Delta t_i},
\qquad
\hat\sigma^2 = \frac1N\sum_i \frac{(\Delta Y_i-\hat\theta\,\Delta t_i)^2}{\Delta t_i},
$$

$$
\hat\mu = \hat\theta + \tfrac12\hat\sigma^2.
$$

**Ornstein-Uhlenbeck** (source: 04_ornstein_uhlenbeck.ipynb):

$$
dX = -\lambda\,(X-\mu)\,dt + \sigma\,dW.
$$

Moments used for fitting:

$$
E[X_t] = X_0 e^{-\lambda t} + \mu\,(1-e^{-\lambda t}),
$$

$$
V[X_t] = \frac{\sigma^2}{2\lambda}\,(1-e^{-2\lambda t}).
$$

## Examples
> [!example] Cancer-cell growth (GBM)
> Counts from Johnson et al. (PLOS Biology), three seeding levels (about 2, 4, 10 cells), file `cancer_growth.csv`. Fit on the 10-cell data for $t<250$ h with zeros replaced by $10^{-6}$: $\hat\mu=0.010$, $\hat\sigma=0.073$. Initial conditions are lognormal, fitted from data. Prediction to 350 h from 1000 simulations (median, 95% interval). The median follows the trend but samples do not look like individual replicates, so a richer model is needed.

> [!example] Treasury-yield recovery (OU)
> FRED series DGS2 (2-year Treasury), recovery after the COVID shock (start 2020-03-12, recovery from 2021-09-12). $x_0$ and $\mu$ set empirically. $\lambda$ is fitted by Adam on mean-squared error against $E[X_t]$ in time rescaled to $[0,1]$, giving $\lambda=5.226$; $\sigma=1.311$ from the variance formula. 1000 Euler-Maruyama paths, median agrees with the mean.

> [!example] Replicates
> With $R$ replicate series the MLEs pool: sums over $r$ in numerator and denominator, with $RN$ in the variance denominator.

**Wiki note (book errors)**:
- 03: the text says $\Delta X\sim N(\mu X\Delta t,\ \sigma^2X\Delta t)$; the variance should be $\sigma^2X^2\Delta t$.
- 04: `estimate_sigma` computes `sqrt((2*lam*var)/1 - exp(-2*lam*t))`; the intended formula is $\sqrt{2\lambda\,\mathrm{var}/(1-e^{-2\lambda t})}$. Also $\lambda$ is in rescaled time, so it is not comparable to a per-day rate.
- 02: a product-of-sines field vanishing on all edges is a pinned (pillow-type) field. The standard Brownian sheet has a different KL basis and is zero only on the two axes **[unverified]**.
