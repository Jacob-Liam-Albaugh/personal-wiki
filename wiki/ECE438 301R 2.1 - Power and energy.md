# ECE438 301R 2.1 - Power and energy

**Summary**: ECE 301 review note on the energy and power of a signal, in continuous and discrete time. Energy is a total, power is energy per unit time, and a signal falls in one of three energy/power categories (source: Topic 2.1 power and energy Jul 17, 2022.pdf, pp. 1-6).

**Sources**: `Digital Signal Processing/301 Review/Topic 2.1 power and energy Jul 17, 2022.pdf` (reading: OWN section 1.1.2).
**Last updated**: 2026-11-06

---

## Definitions

Energy is a total, measured in joules or kWh. Power is average energy per time, measured in watts (watt = 1 joule/second). The signal itself may be in amps, so these are "energy-like" quantities (source: Topic 2.1, p. 1).

Power and energy are always real, since
$$|x(t)|^2 = x(t)\,x^*(t) = x_{Re}^2(t) + x_{Im}^2(t).$$
For real $x(t)$, $|x(t)|^2 = x^2(t)$ (source: Topic 2.1, p. 3).

## Continuous time

Energy between $t_0$ and $t_1$:
$$E = \int_{t_0}^{t_1} |x(t)|^2\,dt$$

Total energy:
$$E_\infty = \lim_{T\to\infty}\int_{-T}^{T} |x(t)|^2\,dt$$

Average power between $t_0$ and $t_1$:
$$P = \frac{E}{t_1 - t_0}$$

Overall average power:
$$P_\infty = \lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{2T}\int_{-T}^{T} |x(t)|^2\,dt$$

Instantaneous power is $|x(t)|^2$ (source: Topic 2.1, pp. 1-4).

## Discrete time

Energy between $n_1$ and $n_2$:
$$E = \sum_{n=n_1}^{n_2} |x[n]|^2$$

Total energy:
$$E_\infty = \lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{n=-N}^{N} |x[n]|^2$$

Average power over the window, and overall:
$$P = \frac{E}{n_2 - n_1 + 1}$$
$$P_\infty = \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{2N+1}\sum_{n=-N}^{N} |x[n]|^2$$

Instantaneous power is $|x[n]|^2$ (source: Topic 2.1, p. 3-4).

A finite-energy CT signal is square-integrable; a finite-energy DT signal is square-summable (source: Topic 2.1, p. 3).

## Example

> [!example] Complex exponential on $(-1/2, 1)$
> $x(t)=\cos t + j\sin t$. Since $|x|^2=\cos^2 t+\sin^2 t = 1$, $E=\int_{-1/2}^{1}dt = 3/2$ and $P = (3/2)/(3/2) = 1$ (source: Topic 2.1, p. 5).

## Three categories

- If total energy is finite, overall average power is 0.
- If overall average power is nonzero, total energy is infinite.
- So the cases are: $E_\infty=\infty,\,P_\infty=\infty$; $E_\infty=\infty,\,P_\infty$ finite; $E_\infty$ finite, $P_\infty=0$. The case finite energy with infinite power cannot happen (source: Topic 2.1, p. 5).

Wiki note: periodic signals have infinite energy and finite power, see [[ECE438 301R 2.3 - Period of a signal]]. The energy of a [[Discrete-time signal]] reappears as Parseval's relation for the [[DTFT]] and [[DFT]].
