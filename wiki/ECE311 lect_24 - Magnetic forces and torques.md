# ECE311 lect_24 - Magnetic forces and torques

**Summary**: Force between current-carrying wires, forces on permeable materials, and torque on a motor coil.

**Course**: ECE 311 (Electromagnetics I, Purdue, Spring 2024, Prof. D. S. Elliott) · **Lecture date**: Mar 4 (per the schedule) · **File**: `Electromagnetics/Lectures Slides/lect_24.pdf` (8 slides)

---

## Topics
- Force per length between parallel wires
- Magnetic levitation example
- Torque on a coil

## Key results
Force per unit length between wires 1 and 2 separated by $d$ (p. 1):
$$F_m'=\frac{\mu_0I_1I_2}{2\pi d}$$

## Examples
> [!example] Levitating a wire (p. 1)
> Two parallel wires, 1 cm apart, carry equal and opposite current $I$. With 22 gauge copper (density $8.96\times10^3$ kg/m$^3$, area $3.26\times10^{-7}$ m$^2$) the weight per length is $F_g'=0.0286$ N/m; setting $F_m'=F_g'$ gives $I=\sqrt{2\pi dF_g'/\mu_0}=37.8$ A (PDF p. 2, text layer). The slide notes this is a huge current for 22 gauge wire; the dissipation is $P'=I^2/\sigma S=76$ W/m.
