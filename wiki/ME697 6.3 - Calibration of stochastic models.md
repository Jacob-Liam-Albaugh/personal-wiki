# ME697 6.3 - Calibration of stochastic models

**Summary**: Estimate the unknown parameters of a stochastic state-space model while reconstructing its state. Two routes on the stochastic Duffing oscillator: expectation-maximization (point estimate) and particle MCMC (posterior samples).
**File**: `advanced-scientific-machine-learning/book/sinverse/cali/`

## Key results

**EM** (source: 02_em_example.ipynb). Given the current iterate $\theta^{(k)}$:

$$
Q(\theta\mid\theta^{(k)}) = E_{p_{\theta^{(k)}}(x_{1:T}\mid y_{1:T})}\big[\log p_\theta(y_{1:T},x_{1:T})\big].
$$

$$
\theta^{(k+1)} = \arg\max_\theta\ Q(\theta\mid\theta^{(k)}).
$$

The expectation is taken over smoothed trajectories (`BootstrapSmoother`); the M-step is run with gradient ascent.

**Particle MCMC** (source: 04_pmcmc_example.ipynb). Propose $\theta'\sim q(\cdot\mid\theta^{(i-1)})$, run a particle filter for the likelihood estimate $\hat Z'$, accept with probability

$$
\alpha = \min\Big(1,\ 
\frac{\hat Z'\,p(\theta')\,q(\theta^{(i-1)}\mid\theta')}
{\hat Z^{(i-1)}\,p(\theta^{(i-1)})\,q(\theta'\mid\theta^{(i-1)})}\Big).
$$

## Examples
> [!example] EM on the Duffing oscillator
> Unknowns $\theta=[\alpha,\beta,\delta,\sigma]$. Start $\alpha_0=\beta_0=\delta_0=0$, $\sigma_0=2$. Settings: 1000 particles, 100 EM iterations, 100 M-step iterations, learning rate $10^{-3}$, `BootstrapSmoother(1)`.
> Estimates: $\alpha=-0.91$ (true $-1$), $\beta=0.93$ (true $1$), $\delta=0.38$ (true $0.3$). The fourth entry is reported as $0.15$ versus "true 0.01" (source: 02_em_example.ipynb). It gives a point estimate only.

> [!example] PMCMC on the same model
> Flat prior, start $\alpha_0=-1.5$, $\beta_0=1.5$, $\delta_0=0.5$, $s_0=1.0$. 1000 particles, proposal scale 0.01, 10,000 iterations, 381.48 s. "Not amazing, but the true parameters are in the range of the posterior." Much more expensive than EM. The notebook asks whether the system is identifiable (source: 04_pmcmc_example.ipynb).

## Connections
- EM gives a point estimate (MLE or MAP-like), PMCMC gives the posterior. Compare with [[Variational inference]] and [[Laplace approximation]] for deterministic models.
- PMCMC is a [[Markov chain Monte Carlo]] method whose likelihood is replaced by the [[Particle filter]] estimate; see also [[Metropolis-Hastings correction]].
- Identifiability of $\sigma$ versus observation noise: [[Identifiability]].
