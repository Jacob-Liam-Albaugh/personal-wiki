# ME697 4.1 - Classical and Bayesian inverse problems

**Summary**: Poses the inverse problem two ways. The classical way fits a model $f$ to data by least squares and gives one answer. The Bayesian way gives a posterior distribution, the "gold standard" of the course. The [[Laplace approximation]] is the first cheap route from one to the other, worked through on a [[Catalysis problem]] example.

**File**: `advanced-scientific-machine-learning/book/inverse/basics/`

---

## Topics
- **01 Classic formulation**: a model $f: X\to Y$, a loss $\ell$, and
  $$\min_x \|f(x)-y\|_2^2.$$
  Case 1: ODEs and simple PDEs, solved with JAX, autodiff and L-BFGS-B. Case 2: legacy codes, which need a differentiable surrogate first. Shortcomings: ill-posed, no uncertainty, no way to use prior knowledge.
- **02 Classic example**: the catalysis ODE fitted with Adam and diffrax (see [[Catalysis problem]]).
- **03 Bayesian formulation**: Bayes' rule with a Gaussian likelihood $\mathcal N(y|f(x),\Sigma)$ and evidence $p(y)=\int p(y|x)p(x)dx$. See [[Bayesian inverse problems]].
- **04 [[Laplace approximation]], general**: Taylor-expand $\log p$ at the mode; the posterior Hessian is $\frac1{\sigma^2}J^TJ-\frac1{\sigma^2}\sum_i(y_i-f_i)\nabla^2f_i+\gamma^{-2}I$.
- **05 Laplace example**: catalysis with $\sigma=5$, $\gamma=10$, and noise estimated with a Jeffreys prior $p(\sigma)\propto1/\sigma$.

## Key results
- The Bayesian solution always exists, is unique, quantifies uncertainty, and is conditional on the model being correct (source: basics/03).
- Three ways to compute it: analytically, by sampling ([[Markov chain Monte Carlo]], see [[ME697 4.2 - Sampling posteriors]]), or by optimization ([[Variational inference]], see [[ME697 4.3 - Variational inference]]).
- With a flat prior on $\theta=\log\sigma$ (equivalent to Jeffreys on $\sigma$), the noise level can be estimated along with the rates by change of variables (source: basics/05).
- Mixed second derivatives between the rates and $\theta$ carry a factor $e^{-2\theta}$ times the residual-Jacobian product (source: basics/05).

> [!failure] Wiki notes: errors in the notebooks
> - basics/05 text gives $\partial^2L/\partial x_j\partial\theta=+2e^{-2\theta}\sum_i(y_i-f_i)\partial f_i/\partial x_j$ for the log posterior $L$. > - basics/05 code: `var_post_sigma` uses the fixed-noise covariance, not the noise-augmented one; it prints "variance" but computes a standard deviation; samples use `mu`, not `mu_noise`.
> - The markdown says $x\in\mathbb R^5$ but the code vectors have 6 entries (the sixth is prior-only).
