# ME697 2.2 - Polynomial chaos

**Summary**: Expand a model $f(\Xi)$ of random inputs in orthonormal polynomials of $\Xi$ ([[Polynomial chaos]]). Mean and variance read off the coefficients; for ODEs a Galerkin projection turns the stochastic ODE into a deterministic ODE for the coefficients. Covers the Hilbert-space background, orthogonal polynomials for any weight, tensor-product bases with sparse-grid quadrature, and limits.

**File**: `advanced-scientific-machine-learning/book/up/polynomial_chaos/`

---

## Topics
- **00 Intro**: PC propagates a small number of independent uncertain inputs through ODEs or PDEs.
- **01 Functional analysis (no proofs)**: vector space, inner product, norm, Cauchy sequences, completeness, Hilbert space, separability, orthonormal basis. Reference: Kreyszig, *Introductory Functional Analysis* (sections 1.1-3.7). See [[Polynomial chaos]] for the setup.
- **02 Uniform inputs**: Gram-Schmidt on $\{1,\xi,\dots,\xi^9\}$ in sympy under $\langle f,g\rangle=\int_0^1fg\,d\xi$. Gives shifted Legendre-type polynomials (the Legendre ones live on $[-1,1]$). High orders show numerical noise.
- **03 Gaussian inputs**: same procedure with the weight $e^{-\xi^2/2}/\sqrt{2\pi}$ gives the Hermite polynomials.
- **04 Numerical orthogonal polynomials**: Gautschi's ORTHPOL idea via the `orthojax` package, for any weight (uniform on $[0,2]$, Gaussian, a two-component Gaussian mixture). Projection of a function, mean and variance of $Z=\sin(2\Xi)/(2\Xi)$ vs Monte Carlo.
- **05 PC for an ODE in 1D**: Galerkin system for the coefficients; exponential decay example.
- **06 Tensor products**: many independent inputs, multi-indices, total-degree truncation, Smolyak sparse-grid quadrature, mixed and dependent inputs.
- **07 PCE of a dynamical system**: vector state; Duffing oscillator with random initial state; PC as a surrogate.
- **08 Limitations**.

## Key results
> [!theorem] Orthonormal expansion (01)
> $\mathcal L^2(\Xi)$ with $\langle f,g\rangle=\mathbb E[f(\Xi)g(\Xi)]$ is a separable Hilbert space for the standard distributions, so it has an orthonormal basis $\{\phi_n\}$ and
> $$f=\sum_n c_n\phi_n,\qquad c_n=\mathbb E[f(\Xi)\phi_n(\Xi)]$$
> $$\|f\|^2=\sum_n c_n^2\quad\text{(Parseval)}$$

> [!theorem] Monic orthogonal polynomials (04)
> For any weight there is a unique monic family $\pi_k=\xi^k+\dots$ with $\langle\pi_k,\pi_l\rangle=0$, $k\ne l$, obeying the three-term recurrence
> $$\pi_{k+1}=(\xi-\alpha_k)\pi_k-\beta_k\pi_{k-1}$$
> $$\alpha_k=\frac{\langle\xi\pi_k,\pi_k\rangle}{\langle\pi_k,\pi_k\rangle}$$
> $$\beta_k=\frac{\langle\pi_k,\pi_k\rangle}{\langle\pi_{k-1},\pi_{k-1}\rangle}$$
> with $\pi_{-1}=0$, $\pi_0=1$. The book calls the orthonormalized family "polynomial chaos"; the name has nothing to do with chaos theory.

> [!algorithm] PC for an ODE (05)
> For $\dot x=f(t,x;\Xi)$, $X_t=\sum_i\alpha_i(t)e_i(\Xi)$. Project the ODE on $e_i$:
> $$\dot\alpha_i=g_i(t,\alpha)=\big\langle f(t,\textstyle\sum_j\alpha_je_j;\cdot),e_i\big\rangle$$
> $$\alpha_i(0)=\langle x_0,e_i\rangle$$
> Truncate at $N$ and raise $N$ until converged. The book notes this is a Galerkin projection. Inner products are done by quadrature.

- Statistics come free: $\mathbb E[X_t]=\alpha_0(t)$ and $\mathbb V[X_t]=\sum_{i\ge1}\alpha_i(t)^2$ (05).
- Multi-d: $\mathcal L^2(\Xi)=\mathcal L^2(\Xi_1)\otimes\dots\otimes\mathcal L^2(\Xi_d)$, basis
$$\phi_\alpha(\xi)=\prod_{i=1}^d\phi_{\alpha_i}(\xi_i),\qquad|\alpha|\le\rho.$$
- Vector states: $\psi_{i,\alpha}=\mathbf e_i\phi_\alpha$ is an orthonormal basis of $\mathcal L^2(\Xi,\mathbb R^n)$ (07).
- Quadrature in $d$ dimensions: full tensor grids explode, so use Smolyak sparse grids built from nested rules (Clenshaw-Curtis/Fejer for Legendre, Gauss-Hermite for Hermite) (06).
- Mixed inputs: map each to uniform by $\Xi_i=F_i^{-1}(U_i)$ and use Legendre; or chaospy's sparse grids (not in JAX). Correlated Gaussians: $\Xi=\mu+LZ$ with $\Sigma=LL^T$; general case: Rosenblatt transformation (06).
- The PC solution is also a **surrogate**: evaluate $\sum\alpha(t)\phi_\alpha(\xi)$ at any $\xi$ with no ODE solve, and get densities or [[Sobol indices]] from it (05, 07).
- Limits (08): the number of terms grows quickly with the number of inputs (curse of dimensionality); poor convergence for discontinuities, singularities, high-frequency oscillation, and chaotic systems. Ref: Bilionis et al. 2013 (J. Comput. Phys.).

## Examples
> [!example] Exponential decay (05)
> $\dot x=-(0.5+0.1\Xi)x$, $x_0=1$, $\Xi\sim\mathcal U[-1,1]$, Legendre degree 5, Tsit5 on the coefficient system, compared with Monte Carlo.

> [!example] Duffing with random initial state (07)
> $x(0),v(0)$ Gaussian, written as $\mu+\sigma\Phi^{-1}((\Xi+1)/2)$ with $\Xi_i\sim\mathcal U[-1,1]$. Degree 5, sparse-grid level 5. PC mean and variance match Monte Carlo and are much faster; accuracy degrades as the initial variance grows.

> [!example] Mean and variance of $\sin(2\Xi)/(2\Xi)$ (04)
> $\Xi\sim\mathcal N(0,1)$: project on Hermite, $\mathbb E[Z]=f_0$, $\mathbb V[Z]=\sum_{k\ge1}f_k^2$, compare Monte Carlo.

> [!example] Homework 3 (source: homework/03_homework.ipynb)
> Pythagoras in Hilbert spaces and $H\cong\ell^2$; orthogonal polynomials for $\Xi\sim\mathrm{Exp}(1)$ with `orthojax`, projection of $\sin$; Lorenz ($\sigma=10$, $\beta=8/3$, $\rho=28$, $x\sim N(0,0.1)$, $y\sim N(5,0.1)$, $z\sim N(20,0.1)$) with a [[Polynomial chaos]] surrogate against 100,000 Monte Carlo samples.

## Wiki notes (possible errors in the book)
- **Inner product (01)**: property 3 is labelled "linearity in the second argument" but writes linearity in the first. Harmless given symmetry.
- **Inner product (06, 07)**: $\mathcal L^2$ is defined with $d\mathbb P$ in 06, but 07 writes $\int g\cdot h\,d\Xi$ (Lebesgue); it must carry the density of $\Xi$ for orthonormality to hold.
- **Uniform range (06)**: the dependent-Gaussian recipe says $U$ is uniform on $[-1,1]$, but $\Phi^{-1}(U)$ needs $U\in[0,1]$. Notebook 07 does it correctly with $(\Xi+1)/2$.
- **"Three choices" (06)**: only two are listed.
- **Growth of terms (08)**: with total degree $\rho$, the number of terms is $\binom{d+\rho}{\rho}$, polynomial in $d$ for fixed $\rho$ and exponential only if $\rho$ grows with $d$. The book's "exponential in the number of variables" is loose.
- **03**: the set $\mathcal L^2$ is written without the condition $\mathbb E[f^2]<\infty$.

## Connections
- First-order local analysis ([[ME697 2.1 - Sensitivity analysis]]) is the degree-1 case; PC keeps nonlinearity.
- Orthonormal basis expansion in random variables is the same device as [[Karhunen-Loève expansion]] (basis in a random field) used in [[ME697 3.1 - Functional inputs]].
