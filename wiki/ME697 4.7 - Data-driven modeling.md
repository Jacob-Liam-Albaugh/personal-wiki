# ME697 4.7 - Data-driven modeling

**Summary**: Learning dynamics from data. [[LASSO]] gives sparse regression, which [[SINDy]] applies to find governing equations by regressing time derivatives on a library of candidate terms. Neural ODEs are announced but empty.

**File**: `advanced-scientific-machine-learning/book/inverse/data/`

---

## Topics
- **01 LASSO**: loss
  $$\frac1N\sum_n(y_n-w_0-w^T\phi_n)^2+\lambda\|w\|_1$$
  with the bias $w_0$ unregularized. Data from $0.2x+0.3x^2+0.4x^3$ with noise 0.01 at 100 points. 20 features: monomials $x^0..x^9$ and $\sin(2\pi ix)$, $i=0..9$.
- **02 SINDy 1**: replicates Brunton et al. (2016), appendix. Fifth-degree polynomial library, `LassoCV` per state: 2D linear, 2D cubic, and 3D linear systems.
- **03 SINDy 2**: the Lorenz system with sequentially thresholded least squares (threshold 0.025), then noisy derivatives.
- **04 Neural ODEs**: a title only (see [[Neural ODE]]).

## Key results
- `LassoCV` gives a sparser model than `Ridge` with cross-validated strength (source: data/01). See [[LASSO]].
- Noise-free linear and cubic systems are identified almost perfectly by `LassoCV` (source: data/02).
- On Lorenz, `LassoCV` failed even after scaling (too many iterations, poor results), so the author switched to sequentially thresholded least squares from Brunton and Kutz (2019). The identified coefficients "look perfect" (source: data/03).
- Trajectories of the chaotic learned model diverge from truth after a while, but the attractor matches, also with noise $\eta=10$ added to the derivatives (source: data/03).

## Connections
- The Laplace prior view of L1 links to [[Bayesian inverse problems]]: the penalty is the negative log posterior under a Laplace prior.
- Wiki note: sparse regression is the optimization problem of [[ME697 1.4 - Optimization]] with a non-smooth term.
- Forward-simulating the identified model uses the ODE solvers of [[ME697 4.5 - ODE inverse problems]].
