# ME697 2.6 - Symmetries

**Summary**: Physical laws do not depend on the origin or orientation of the coordinate system. This lecture builds the group theory needed to say so (groups, homomorphisms, representations, the Euclidean group $E(n)=T(n)\rtimes O(n)$), defines invariance and [[Equivariance]], and shows a Euclidean neural network ([[e3nn]]) classifying 3D tetris pieces after rotation and translation, trained on one orientation only.

**File**: `advanced-scientific-machine-learning/book/up/symmetries/`

---

## Topics
- **Intro**: embedding known symmetries lets a surrogate learn with less data.
- **01 Symmetries**: groups and examples; homomorphisms; groups of transformations; representations; the Euclidean group; semidirect products; invariance; equivariance; pointer to Euclidean networks (Geiger et al. 2022).
- **02 E3NN (hands-on)**: tetris pieces as graphs, spherical harmonics and irreps, an e3nn-jax graph network, training and a rotated/translated test.

## Key results
> [!definition] Group (01)
> A set $G$ with $G\times G\to G$, $(g_1,g_2)\mapsto g_1g_2$, with closure, associativity, identity $e$, and inverses. Examples: $(\mathbb Z,+)$, $(\mathbb Q\setminus\{0\},\times)$, $(\mathbb R,+)$, the translation group $T(n)$, the general linear group $GL(n,\mathbb R)$, and
> $$SO(n)=\{A: AA^{T}=I,\ \det A=1\}$$
> $$O(n)=\{A: AA^{T}=I\}$$
> with $SO(n)\le O(n)\le GL(n)$. Elements of $O(n)$ outside $SO(n)$ are reflections. Rotations in 3D do not commute ($R_1R_2\neq R_2R_1$).

- **Homomorphism**: $\phi(g_1g_2)=\phi(g_1)\phi(g_2)$; bijective means isomorphism. Examples: $x\mapsto x\bmod 3$ from $\mathbb Z$ to $\mathbb Z_3$ (not injective); $x\mapsto e^x$ from $(\mathbb R,+)$ to $(\mathbb R^+,\times)$ (isomorphism).
- **Group of transformations**: bijections of a set $V$ under composition. **Representation**: matrices for group elements, e.g. $GL(V)\cong GL(n)$ via a basis.

> [!definition] Euclidean group (01)
> Isometries of $\mathbb R^n$ (affine maps preserving distance):
> $$E(n)\cong T(n)\rtimes O(n)$$
> Elements are $(\mathbf b,A)$ with composition
> $$(\mathbf b_1,A_1)(\mathbf b_2,A_2)=(\mathbf b_1+A_1\mathbf b_2,\ A_1A_2)$$

> [!theorem] Semidirect product (01)
> If $H,K\le G$, $H\cap K=\{e\}$, $G=HK$ uniquely, and $H$ is normal in $G$, then $G\cong H\rtimes K$ with
> $$(h_1,k_1)(h_2,k_2)=(h_1\,k_1h_2k_1^{-1},\ k_1k_2)$$
> Proof idea: insert $k_1^{-1}k_1$ in $h_1k_1h_2k_2$. For $E(n)$: write $f=t_{\mathbf b}\,g$ with $\mathbf b=f(\mathbf 0)$ and $g$ orthogonal; $T(n)$ is normal; $T(n)\cap O(n)=\{e\}$.

> [!definition] Invariance and equivariance (01)
> $f$ is invariant under $G$ if
> $$f(g\mathbf x)=f(\mathbf x)$$
> and equivariant if
> $$f(g\mathbf x)=g\,f(\mathbf x)$$
> with $g$ acting on the input and output spaces by possibly different representations. Example: atom positions $\mathbf r_i$ in, forces $\mathbf f_i$ out. Under $g=(\mathbf b,A)\in E(3)$, positions go to $A\mathbf r_i+\mathbf b$ but forces only to $A\mathbf f_i$ (translation leaves forces unchanged).

- **Invariant by averaging (finite $G$)**: from any $h_\theta$,
$$f_\theta(\mathbf x)=\sum_{g\in G}h_\theta(g\mathbf x)$$
  is invariant. For continuous $G$ the sum becomes a Haar integral $\int_G h_\theta(g\mathbf x)\,dg$, usually hard (Lie groups).

> [!algorithm] E3NN tetris classifier (02)
> 1. Each piece is four points in $\mathbb R^3$; edges connect points within radius 1.1 (`e3nn.radius_graph`); graphs built with `jraph`. Eight classes, including a chiral pair, square, line, corner, L, T, zigzag.
> 2. Edge features: spherical harmonics $Y_\ell^m$ of the relative position, $\ell\le 3$, tensor-multiplied with sender features. The continuous filter is
> $$W(\vec r)=R(r)\,Y_\ell^m(\hat r)$$
> with learned radial function $R$.
> 3. Features are typed by irreps (`1e`, `2o`, ...: degree $\ell$ and parity even/odd); `e3nn.flax.Linear` mixes only irreps of equal type.
> 4. Three hidden layers `32x0e + 32x0o + 8x1e + 8x1o + 8x2e + 8x2o`, output `0o + 7x0e`, scatter-sum over nodes, softmax cross-entropy, [[Adam]] at 0.01 for up to 100 steps.
> 5. Trained on one orientation of each piece. A test piece rotated 90 degrees about $z$ and translated is classified correctly.
> (source: `02_e3nn.ipynb`)

## Wiki notes (possible errors in the book)
- **Action vs composition (01)**: the text says $(\mathbf b,A)\mathbf x=A(\mathbf x+\mathbf b)$ and "a translation followed by a rotation", but the composition law $(\mathbf b_1+A_1\mathbf b_2,A_1A_2)$ is that of $\mathbf x\mapsto A\mathbf x+\mathbf b$ (rotate first, then translate).
- **Normality proof (01)**: the book gets $ft_{\mathbf b}f^{-1}=t_{f(\mathbf b)}$. For $f(\mathbf x)=A\mathbf x+\mathbf c$ it is $t_{A\mathbf b}$, because the translation part $\mathbf c$ cancels. The conclusion (a translation) is unaffected.
- **Typo (01)**: "$g(\mathbf x)=t_{\mathbf b}^{-1}f(\mathbf x)=\mathbf x=f(x)-\mathbf b$" has a stray "$=\mathbf x$"; and "$f$ is a homomorphism" appears where $\phi$ is meant.
- **Linear layer inference (02)**: the notebook says the output lacks `1e` because the network "learned" that it is unimportant. In e3nn a `Linear` layer only connects irreps present in the input, and the input `2o + 3o` has no `1e`, so it drops out by construction, not by training.
- **Chirality (02)**: the text says the network is "invariant to rotations, reflections and translations", but the pieces include a mirror-image pair. The readout uses an odd scalar (`0o`) times an even scalar, giving logits $\pm\,\text{odd}\cdot\text{even}$, which flips sign under reflection and so separates the chiral pair. A reflection-invariant network could not.
- **Thin test (02)**: only one transformed piece is checked, and `test_transform` hard-codes the label 5 (unused in the prediction). The "reflection across the $y$ axis" example flips $y\to-y$, a reflection across the $xz$ plane.
- A pinned `jax==0.4.33` is needed because e3nn breaks with 0.4.34 at the time of writing.
