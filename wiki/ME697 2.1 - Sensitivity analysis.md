# ME697 2.1 - Sensitivity analysis

**Summary**: Opens Part 2 (uncertainty propagation). [[Sensitivity analysis]] in two forms: local (linearize the ODE solution in its parameters, get a Gaussian process from one Jacobian), and global ([[Sobol indices]] from quasi-random designs such as [[Latin hypercube sampling]] and Sobol sequences). Includes the [[Fokker-Planck equation]] as the exact (but unscalable) alternative.

**File**: `advanced-scientific-machine-learning/book/up/sensitivity_analysis/`

---

## Topics
- **Part 2 intro (`up/intro.md`)**: uncertainty propagation = quantify input uncertainty, push it through a model, "more efficiently than Monte Carlo". Assumes probability and Monte Carlo background.
- **01 Theory (local)**: IVP $\dot x=f(x,t,\theta)$, $x(0)=x_0(\theta)$, with $\theta\approx\mathcal N(\mu,\Sigma)$. Needs small uncertainty, $f$ differentiable in $\theta$, and a non-chaotic system. First-order Taylor expansion of $x(t;\theta)$ about $\mu$ makes $X(t)$ affine in $\theta$, hence Gaussian.
- **02 Differentiating ODE solutions**: two ways to get $\nabla_\theta x$: autodiff through a JAX solver (Diffrax), or a second ODE for the sensitivity ("method of adjoints" in the book). See [[Local sensitivity analysis of ODEs]].
- **03 Duffing oscillator**: local analysis with `jax.jacobian`, covariance by one `einsum`.
- **04 Lorenz system**: local analysis fails for chaos.
- **05 Fokker-Planck**: transport equation for the density of $x(t)$ under random initial conditions; exponential-decay example.
- **06 [[Latin hypercube sampling]]**: stratified samples, convergence of the variance estimate vs plain Monte Carlo.
- **07 Sobol sequence**: low-discrepancy, extensible; compared with LHS by discrepancy.
- **08 Global sensitivity**: first-order and total [[Sobol indices]] of the Duffing oscillator with SALib.

## Key results
> [!theorem] Local propagation (01)
> To first order the solution is a vector-valued [[Gaussian process]]:
> $$X \sim \mathrm{GP}\big(x(t;\mu),\ c(t,t')\big)$$
> $$c(t,t')=\nabla_\theta x(t;\mu)\,\Sigma\,\nabla_\theta x(t';\mu)^T$$
> The covariance is matrix valued (state components by state components). One solve plus one Jacobian replaces many Monte Carlo runs.

> [!algorithm] Sensitivity IVP (02)
> Differentiating the ODE in $\theta$ gives a linear ODE for $S=\nabla_\theta x$:
> $$\dot S=\nabla_x f\,S+\nabla_\theta f$$
> $$S(0)=\nabla_\theta x_0(\theta)$$
> Solve the original IVP first, then this one with the same solver. The book says adjoint-based gradients are cheaper but possibly less accurate than autodiff through the solver, and that either is fine here.

> [!theorem] Fokker-Planck for random initial data (05)
> For $\dot x=f(x,t)$, $x_0\sim p_0$, the density of $X_t$ obeys
> $$\partial_t p=-\nabla\cdot(f\,p),\qquad p(x,0)=p_0(x).$$
> Derived from a flux balance on a volume $V$ and the divergence theorem. The book stresses it is not the general form (that needs SDEs) and is hopeless to solve in high dimension.

- Local analysis tracks Monte Carlo well for a while on Duffing (5% parameter noise) and then drifts, "expected whenever the solution is not a linear function of the parameters" (03).
- For Lorenz ($\sigma=10$, $\beta=8/3$, $\rho=28$) a blob of initial conditions with std $10^{-3}$ spreads over the whole attractor; the local mean and variance fail quickly, the variance faster than the mean. The Jacobian also fills with NaNs late in time (float32 overflow) (04).
- LHS converges faster than uniform sampling for the variance of $y(t)$, but a single run is noisy; repeating ~100 times gives error bars on the error (06).
- Sobol sequences are naturally extendable; adding points to an LHS design breaks its stratification. Discrepancy is the measure of space-filling (07).
- Duffing, $\pm1\%$ parameter ranges, $N=512$: early output variance is dominated by $\gamma$, later by $\omega$. First-order indices often do not sum to 1, so interactions matter; total indices sum to $\ge1$ (08).

## Examples
> [!example] Duffing oscillator (03, 08)
> $$\ddot x+\delta\dot x+\alpha x+\beta x^3=\gamma\cos(\omega t)$$
> Nominal $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta,\omega)=(1,5,0.37,0.1,1)$, zero initial state, $t\in[0,50]$, Tsit5 in Diffrax. Converges to a limit cycle.

> [!example] Exponential decay (05)
> $\dot x=-\lambda x$, $x_0\sim\mathcal N(\mu_0,\sigma_0^2)$ gives $\partial_t p=\lambda\,\partial_x(x\,p)$ and a Gaussian $p$, checked against Monte Carlo ($\mu_0=2$, $\sigma_0=0.2$, $\lambda=2$).

> [!example] Random decay rate (06)
> $\dot y=-ay$, $a=0.1x_1$, $y_0=8+2x_2$, $x_i\sim\mathcal U(0,1)$. Exact moments:
> $$\mathbb E[y]=\tfrac{90}{t}\,(1-e^{-0.1t})$$
> $$\mathbb V[y]=\tfrac{2440}{6t}\,(1-e^{-0.2t})-\mathbb E[y]^2$$
> Wiki note: both check out by direct integration.

> [!example] Homework 2 (source: homework/02_homework.ipynb)
> Duffing oscillator with $\alpha=1$, $\beta=5$, $\gamma=8$, $\delta=0.02$, $\omega=0.5$ for the strange attractor. Local sensitivity with $\theta\sim N(\mu,\Sigma)$ gives credible intervals; a Gaussian-process view; then [[Sobol indices]] on $t\in[0,30]$ step 0.1 after mapping $\theta_i$ to uniforms.

## Wiki notes (possible errors in the book)
- **Fokker-Planck variance (05)**: the book gives $\sigma_t^2=\sigma_0^2e^{-2\lambda t}+\frac{\mu_0^2}{2\lambda}(1-e^{-2\lambda t})$. For $\dot x=-\lambda x$ the map $x_0\mapsto x_0e^{-\lambda t}$ is linear, so the variance is just $\sigma_0^2e^{-2\lambda t}$. The extra term is the stationary variance of an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process, which is not this system. The notebook code uses the wrong formula too, so its density plot would disagree with the Monte Carlo curves.
- **Name**: for a deterministic ODE the equation in 05 is the Liouville (continuity) equation; Fokker-Planck proper has a diffusion term.
- **"Adjoint" (02)**: the IVP for $\nabla_\theta x$ is the forward sensitivity equation. The adjoint method integrates backward in time and is cheaper for many parameters and a scalar output.
- **Sobol indices "always sum to 1" (08)**: only for additive models. Generally first-order indices sum to $\le1$, as the page itself finds afterwards.
- **Sampler note (07)**: the code comment says the number of samples must be a power of 2 for LHS. It is Sobol sequences that need $2^m$ points for their balance properties; LHS works for any $N$.
- **Sobol recursion (07)**: the displayed recursion for $m_{k,j}$ omits the last two terms of the Joe-Kuo form ($2^{s_j}m_{k-s_j}\oplus m_{k-s_j}$) **[unverified]**, and the polynomial coefficient indexing is inconsistent ($a_{j,k}$ vs $a_{k,j}$).
- Typos: Taylor expansion is "about $\mu$" but the text says mean "$\theta$"; the Lorenz notebook reuses `sigma` for both the Lorenz parameter and the initial-condition std.

## Connections
- Local analysis is the first-order case of [[Polynomial chaos]] (ME697 2.2), which keeps the full nonlinearity.
- The Fokker-Planck equation is the deterministic limit of the SDE version used in [[Langevin dynamics]] and [[SGLD]].
- Sobol indices are cheap to read off a PCE; see [[ME697 2.2 - Polynomial chaos]].
- Samples from these designs train a [[Surrogate model]] ([[ME697 2.3 - Surrogates]]).
