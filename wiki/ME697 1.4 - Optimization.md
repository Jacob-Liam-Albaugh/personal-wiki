# ME697 1.4 - Optimization

**Summary**: The optimizers used to train every model in the course: [[Gradient descent]], [[Momentum]], [[SGD]], adaptive rates up to [[Adam]], and [[Newton's method]] with L-BFGS, plus [[Weight initialization]] and training on a GPU with Equinox and optax.

**File**: `advanced-scientific-machine-learning/book/ml-software/optimization/`

---

## Topics
- **01 Problems**: stationary points; a minimum has a positive semidefinite Hessian, a saddle has eigenvalues of both signs. In high dimensions saddles are far more common than local minima, and local minima are usually not the problem. Plateaus (fixed by momentum and adaptive rates), cliffs (gradient clipping), vanishing gradients (initialization, activations). Reading: Goodfellow et al., ch. 8.
- **02 [[Gradient descent]]**, with the Armijo backtracking line search.
- **03 [[Momentum]]**, derived from a damped Newtonian particle; Nesterov.
- **04 optax**: `optimizer.init(params)` gives a state; `optimizer.update(grads, state)` gives updates. Momentum state is a `trace`.
- **05 [[SGD]]**: minibatches, epochs, Robbins-Monro step sizes.
- **06 Adaptive rates**: delta-bar-delta, AdaGrad, RMSProp, [[Adam]], Nadam, AdamW.
- **07 Second order**: [[Newton's method]], BFGS, L-BFGS (`jaxopt.LBFGS`).
- **08 [[Weight initialization]]**: zero, constant, Xavier/Glorot, "He".
- **09 GPU training**: MNIST CNN with Equinox, optax and `eqx.filter_jit`.

## Key results
- The negative gradient is the steepest-descent direction.
- Started exactly on a saddle, gradient descent and momentum stay put; a small initial velocity (in optax, `trace=[0.1, 0]`) escapes.
- Constant-step SGD does not converge; it wanders around the optimum.
- AdaGrad's rate only shrinks, so the book advises against it.
- Newton converges in one step on a quadratic but costs $O(n^3)$ per step. L-BFGS suits small datasets and problems with a unique solution; otherwise use several restarts.
- JAX uses the GPU by default when present; the CNN trained 5-10 times faster than on CPU.

## Examples
> [!example] 1D and 2D test functions (02-03)
> $f(x)=x^4-3x^3+2$ from $x_0=2$, and the saddle function
> $$f(x)=x_0^4-2x_0^2+x_1^2$$
> used for gradient descent, momentum and Nesterov.

> [!example] Quadratic regression (05)
> Equinox model $\theta_0+\theta_1x+\theta_2x^2$ trained by minibatch SGD; constant-rate iterates wiggle around the least-squares fit.

> [!example] Homework 1, problem 5: autoencoder (source: homework/01_homework.ipynb)
> MNIST autoencoder, latent dimension 2, two hidden layers of 128 ReLU units, Equinox and Optax, [[Adam]] at $10^{-3}$, batch 32; then fit a 10-component Gaussian mixture to the latent space and sample from it.
