# ME697 4.2 - Sampling posteriors

**Summary**: [[Markov chain Monte Carlo]] builds posterior samples of all unknowns. The chapter runs Metropolis-Hastings, [[Hamiltonian Monte Carlo]] and [[NUTS]] with Blackjax on a banana-shaped density and shows how to judge the chains (R-hat, effective sample size, burn-in, thinning).

**File**: `advanced-scientific-machine-learning/book/inverse/sampling/`

---

## Topics
- **01 MCMC basics**: a short text that defers to *Bayesian Data Analysis* (Gelman et al.) ch. 11 and to ME 539 Lecture 27 (source: sampling/01).
- **02 Metropolis-Hastings with Blackjax**: `blackjax.rmh` with a Gaussian random-walk proposal, on a banana density (after Wang et al., arXiv 1706.01435, §5.1.3). Five chains of 400 steps, burn-in 100, thinning 4. See [[Metropolis-Hastings correction]].
- **03 HMC with Blackjax**: step size 0.1, 20 leapfrog steps, identity mass matrix. A higher-curvature banana ($b=1$, $\rho=0.9$), five chains of 200, burn-in 50.
- **04 NUTS with Blackjax**: HMC with an adaptive number of steps; `window_adaptation` tunes step size and mass matrix (500 warmup, 500 samples, 4 chains, thin 2).

## Key results
- Diagnostics: split R-hat compares within-chain and between-chain variance (near 1 means mixed); ESS discounts autocorrelation; burn-in removes the transient; thinning reduces storage, not variance. Trace plots come from arviz.
- NUTS is HMC with an adaptive number of steps, and `window_adaptation` tunes step size and mass matrix during warmup (source: sampling/04).
- Wiki note: HMC usually mixes better than random-walk MH on curved targets but needs step size and path length tuned; NUTS removes the path-length choice. **[unverified]** against the notebook outputs, which were not read.

## Examples
> [!example] Banana density (02-04)
> A twisted two-dimensional Gaussian, curved so that axis-aligned proposals mix slowly. The same target is used for all three samplers so the traces are comparable.

> [!failure] Wiki notes: errors in the notebooks
> - sampling/02 says to change the `alpha` parameter, but the proposal's parameter is `sigma`.
> - The R-hat text calls $\hat V$ "an estimate of the variance between chains". Strictly it is the pooled marginal-variance estimate that mixes within-chain and between-chain variance.
