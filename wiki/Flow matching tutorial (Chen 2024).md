# Flow Matching Tutorial (Chen 2024)

**Summary**: A 175-slide NeurIPS 2024 tutorial on flow matching (FM) by three of the method's developers. It builds the method from the simplest linear-path regression up to the general recipe (conditional path, conditional velocity, marginalization, regression loss), then surveys design choices (couplings, symmetries, manifolds), ways to adapt a trained model (faster sampling, inverse problems, reward fine-tuning) and the extension to arbitrary Markov processes (Generator Matching, discrete flows). No experiments of its own; results are cited from the underlying papers.

## Problem

Learn a generative model that turns samples of a source $p$ into samples of a data distribution $q$ by integrating an ODE, trained by plain regression with no simulation during training (slides 4-19).

## Method

### Notation used by the tutorial

$X_0\sim p$ is the source (noise) at $t=0$ and $X_1\sim q$ is data at $t=1$. A flow $\psi_t$ is defined by a velocity field: $\frac{d}{dt}\psi_t(x)=u_t(\psi_t(x))$ (slide 14). $p_t$ is the law of $X_t=\psi_t(X_0)$. This is the opposite time direction to [[Step-by-step diffusion (Nakkiran 2025)]] and to the diffusion papers, which put data at $t=0$.

### 01 Flow matching basics (slides 4-36)

> [!algorithm] Simplest flow matching (slide 19)
> $$X_t=(1-t)X_0+tX_1,\qquad \mathcal L(\theta)=\mathbb E_{t,X_0,X_1}\big\|u_t^\theta(X_t)-(X_1-X_0)\big\|^2$$
> Sample by solving $\dot X_t=u_t^\theta(X_t)$ from $X_0\sim p$ with any ODE solver; the midpoint rule is said to work well (slide 17).

The general recipe (slides 21-24):

- Pick a conditional path $p_{t|1}(x\mid x_1)$ from $p$ to a point mass at $x_1$, generated by a conditional velocity $u_t(x\mid x_1)$.
- Marginal path and velocity: $p_t(x)=\mathbb E_{X_1}\,p_{t|1}(x\mid X_1)$ and $u_t(x)=\mathbb E[u_t(X_t\mid X_1)\mid X_t=x]$.

> [!theorem] Marginalization trick (slide 23)
> The marginal velocity $u_t$ generates the marginal probability path $p_t$.

- Loss: $\mathcal L_\text{FM}=\mathbb E\,D\big(u_t(X_t),u^\theta_t(X_t)\big)$ is intractable; $\mathcal L_\text{CFM}=\mathbb E\,D\big(u_t(X_t\mid X_1),u^\theta_t(X_t)\big)$ is not, and $\nabla_\theta\mathcal L_\text{FM}=\nabla_\theta\mathcal L_\text{CFM}$ (slide 24). The identity holds exactly when $D$ is a Bregman divergence (Holderrieth et al. 2024; slides 25-26); squared error is one.
- The linear ("conditional OT") conditional flow minimizes a bound on kinetic energy, reduces the kinetic energy of the initial coupling and is exact optimal transport for a single data point; the resulting marginal flow is "not Optimal Transport (but in high dim straighter)" (slide 28).
- Affine paths (slide 30): $\psi_t(x\mid x_1)=\alpha_tx_1+\sigma_tx$, with marginal velocity $u_t(x)=\mathbb E[\dot\alpha_tX_1+\dot\sigma_tX_0\mid X_t=x]$. Predicting $X_1$ ("$x_1$-prediction"), $X_0$ ("$x_0$-prediction") or the velocity are equivalent parameterizations, convertible by linear formulas.
- Gaussian paths (slide 31): with $p=\mathcal N(0,I)$ and independent $(X_0,X_1)$, $x_0$-prediction is [[Noise prediction|noise prediction]], $x_1$-prediction is denoising, and the flow is the [[Diffusion models#Probability-flow ODE|probability-flow ODE]] of a diffusion model. FM versus diffusion (slide 33): a diffusion model's path is defined by a forward noising process, with Gaussian source and independent coupling; FM takes any $p$, $q$, coupling and any conditional path it knows how to generate.

### 02 Advanced designs (slides 37-110)

- **Conditioning and guidance** (slides 39-46): train $u_t^\theta(x\mid y)$ with the same loss over $(X_0,X_1,Y)$. For Gaussian paths, [[Classifier guidance]] is $\tilde u_t(x\mid y)=u_t(x)+w\,b_t\nabla\log p_{Y|t}(y\mid x)$ and [[Classifier-free guidance]] is $\tilde u_t(x\mid y)=(1-w)\,u_t(x)+w\,u_t(x\mid y)$ (slide 43). Guidance for non-Gaussian paths is listed as open (slide 46).
- **Data couplings** (slides 47-74): FM works for any joint $\pi_{0,1}$ of $(X_0,X_1)$ with the right marginals. Paired data: start from a corrupted sample, $X_0=Y+\varepsilon$, instead of noise (stochastic interpolants with couplings, Albergo et al. 2024; I2SB; slides 54-61). Multisample (minibatch OT) couplings: pair $k$ source and $k$ data points by optimal transport within the batch; kinetic energy is bounded by $\mathbb E\|X_1-X_0\|^2$, $k=1$ is the independent coupling and $k\to\infty$ recovers the OT map (Pooladian et al. 2023, Tong et al. 2023; slides 63-72). In high dimensions the slides report only a minor gain in sampling speed over tailored samplers; they see promise in lower-dimensional scientific problems such as protein backbone design (slide 72).
- **Symmetries** (slides 76-93): if the source density is invariant under a group and the velocity is equivariant, $u_t(g\cdot x)=g\cdot u_t(x)$, the generated density is invariant. See [[Equivariance]].
- **Riemannian FM** (slides 94-110; Chen and Lipman 2023): on a manifold with metric $g$ the loss is $\mathbb E\|u^\theta_t(X_t)-u_t(X_t\mid X_1)\|_g^2$. On simple manifolds the conditional flow follows geodesics, $\psi_t(x_0\mid x_1)=\exp_{x_0}\!\big(\kappa(t)\log_{x_0}(x_1)\big)$; on general geometries a "premetric" $d$ replaces the geodesic distance, the conditional flow satisfies $d(\psi_t(x_0\mid x_1),x_1)=\bar\kappa(t)\,d(x_0,x_1)$, and training requires simulation (slides 105-107).

### 03 Model adaptation (slides 111-152)

- **Faster sampling** (slides 113-128): rectified flow retrains on the model's own $(X_0,X_1)$ pairs to straighten paths, at a slight cost in sample quality (slides 114-116); shortcut models learn a step-size-conditioned velocity with $X_{t+h}=X_t+h\,s_t(X_t,h)$ and the self-consistency $s_t(X_t,2h)=\tfrac12s_t(X_t,h)+\tfrac12s_t(X_{t+h},h)$ (Frans et al. 2024; slides 117-121; the CFG weight must be fixed before training); a trained Gaussian-path model can be sampled under a different scheduler by the change of variables $\bar X_r=s_rX_{t_r}$ with $s_r=\bar\sigma_r/\sigma_{t_r}$ and $t_r$ matching signal-to-noise ratios, which bespoke solvers optimize; these leave the model unchanged but do not reach distillation quality at very few function evaluations (slides 123-127).
- **Inverse problems, training-free** (slides 129-141). Target the posterior $p_{1|Y}(x_1\mid y)\propto p_1(x_1)\,p_{Y|1}(y\mid x_1)$ with a pre-trained unconditional model:

> [!algorithm] Guided velocity for an inverse problem (slides 131-133)
> For a Gaussian path,
> $$u_t(x\mid y)=u_t(x)+\sigma_t^2\,\frac{d\log(\alpha_t/\sigma_t)}{dt}\,\nabla_{x}\log p_{Y|t}(y\mid x)$$
> The likelihood score at time $t$ is unknown and is approximated (e.g. ΠGDM; Pokle et al. 2024).

> [!failure]
> Stated caveats (slide 134): these methods typically assume a linear corruption and a Gaussian path, rely on a heuristic likelihood approximation, and can fail on random samples.

  The alternative is to optimize the source point through the flow, $\min_{x_0}L(\psi_1^\theta(x_0))$ with e.g. $L(x)=\|f(x)-y\|^2$ (D-Flow, Ben-Hamu et al. 2024; slides 135-140). The flow's Jacobian projects the gradient along the data manifold; the cost is repeated simulation and differentiation through the ODE solve. See [[Diffusion posterior sampling]].
- **Reward fine-tuning** (slides 142-152): maximize $\mathbb E_{X_1\sim p^\theta}[r(X_1)]$ by RL or direct gradients (needs LoRA to stay near the base model; prone to reward hacking; slides 145-146), or by stochastic optimal control targeting the tilted law $p^*(X_1)\propto p^\text{base}(X_1)\exp(r(X_1))$. A KL penalty on paths gives a "value function bias" $p^*(X_{(0,1)})=p^\text{base}(X_{(0,1)})\exp(r(X_1)+V(X_0))$ because $X_0$ and $X_1$ are dependent; fixes are to learn the optimal source (Uehara et al. 2024) or to fine-tune under a memoryless SDE (Adjoint Matching, Domingo-Enrich et al. 2024) (slides 147-151).

### 04 Generator Matching and discrete flows (slides 153-171)

- A continuous-time Markov process (flow, diffusion or jump process) is described by its generator $\mathcal L_t$, the first-order term of the transition kernel: $p_{t+h|t}(\cdot,x)=\delta_x+h\,\mathcal L_t(\cdot,x)+o(h)$ (slides 155-157). The FM recipe carries over: conditional generators average to a marginal generator that generates the marginal path, trained with a Bregman divergence (Generator Matching, Holderrieth et al. 2024; slides 162-164).
- Discrete FM on token sequences (Gat et al. 2024, Campbell et al. 2024; slides 165-168): velocities are rates $u_t(y,x)$ of a continuous-time Markov chain, factorized per token; the mixture path $p^i_{t|1}(x^i\mid x_1)=(1-t)\,p(x^i)+t\,\delta(x^i,x^i_1)$ has conditional rate $\frac{1}{1-t}\delta(y^i,x_1^i)$ for $y^i\ne x^i$.
- Open problems listed (slide 171): going beyond factorized velocities, better sampling, exploring the design space.

### 05 Codebase (slides 173-174)

The `flow_matching` library supports continuous, Riemannian and discrete FM, with training code for ImageNet 32/64 and FineWeb.

## Limitations

- As stated on the slides: marginal linear paths are not optimal transport (slide 28); guidance is only understood for Gaussian paths (slide 46); minibatch OT helps little in high dimension (slide 72); training-free inverse-problem methods are heuristic (slide 134); source optimization is expensive (slide 140).
- Wiki note: slides give statements without proofs or conditions; the companion guide is the place to check them.
