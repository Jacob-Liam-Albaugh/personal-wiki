# ECE438 301R 2.3 - Period of a signal

**Summary**: ECE 301 review note on periodic signals: the definition, finding the period and fundamental period of CT and DT signals, and why not every DT sinusoid is periodic. Periodic signals are the basic collection for linear systems (source: Topic 2.3 period of a signal Note Jul 23, 2022.pdf, p. 1).

## Definition

$x(t)$ is periodic with period $T$ ($T\in\mathbb R$, $T>0$) if
$$x(t-T)=x(t)\quad\text{for all } t.$$
$x[n]$ is periodic with period $N$ ($N\in\mathbb Z$, $N>0$) if
$$x[n-N]=x[n]\quad\text{for all } n.$$
Equivalently $x(t+T)=x(t)$. The fundamental period $T_0$ is the smallest period; it is always positive (source: Topic 2.3, p. 2).

Example: $\sin 2t$ has period $\pi$, so $2\pi$ is also a period, but $T_0=\pi$ (source: Topic 2.3, p. 2).

## Complex exponential

For $x(t)=e^{jat}$ need $e^{jaT}=1$. Since $e^{j2\pi k}=1$ for integer $k$:
$$T=\left|\frac{2\pi k}{a}\right|,\quad k\ne0.$$
$$T_0=\left|\frac{2\pi}{a}\right|,\qquad \omega_0=\frac{2\pi}{T_0}.$$
$\omega_0$ is the fundamental frequency (source: Topic 2.3, p. 3).

## DT sinusoids

> [!example] $x[n]=\sin(3\pi n/4)$
> Write $\omega_1=3\pi/4$. Need $\cos\omega_1N=1$ and $\sin\omega_1N=0$, so $\omega_1N=2\pi k$, giving $N=8k/3$. A DT period must be an integer, so take the smallest integer reached: $8/3,16/3,24/3$ gives $N=8$. Check: $\cos 6\pi=1$, $\sin 6\pi=0$ (source: Topic 2.3, pp. 3-5).

> [!example] $x[n]=\sin(5n)$ is not periodic
> The CT signal $\sin 5t$ has $T=2\pi/5$. The DT period would be $\mathrm{LCM}(1,2\pi/5)$, which does not exist (source: Topic 2.3, p. 6).

So not all DT sinusoids are periodic. $\mathrm{LCM}(a,b)$ is the smallest positive integer divisible by both (source: Topic 2.3, p. 5-6). Wiki note: the DT rule is that $\sin(\omega n)$ is periodic exactly when $\omega/2\pi$ is rational; this is consistent with both examples. See [[ECE438 DSP.1 - DT signals]].

## Assorted facts

- $x(t)=1$ is periodic with many periods, but has no fundamental period.
- Periodic signals have infinite total energy and finite average power, see [[ECE438 301R 2.1 - Power and energy]].
- One counterexample suffices to disprove periodicity.
- A sum $x_1+x_2$ with periods $T_1,T_2$ is periodic only if $\mathrm{LCM}(T_1,T_2)$ exists. Example: $\cos 3t$ and $\sin\pi t$.
- If a complex periodic $x(t)$ has period $T$, then its real and imaginary parts are both periodic with period $T$.

(source: Topic 2.3, p. 7-8)

Periodic signals are the natural input class for Fourier series, see [[Fourier transform]] and [[DFT]].
