# Auto-Encoding Variational Bayes (Kingma, Welling 2014)

**Summary**: Introduces the SGVB estimator, a reparameterized Monte Carlo estimate of the variational lower bound that is differentiable in the variational parameters, and the AEVB algorithm, which uses it to train a recognition (encoder) network jointly with a generative (decoder) model. With neural-network encoder and decoder this is the [[VAE]]. On MNIST and Frey Face it optimizes the bound faster and to better values than wake-sleep (p. 7) and converges faster than Monte Carlo EM in marginal likelihood (p. 8).

**Authors**: Diederik P. Kingma, Max Welling (both Universiteit van Amsterdam, Machine Learning Group) · **Year**: 2014 · **File**: `Bayesian/Kingma Welling - Auto-Encoding Variational Bays - Bayesian Variational Inference & Reparametrization Trick.pdf`

The PDF is the arXiv v10 (1 May 2014). It carries no venue header; ICLR 2014 comes from the citation in the other two papers in this batch ([[Variational dropout (Kingma 2015)]] ref. [14], [[Intro to VAEs (Kingma 2019)]] reference list).

## Problem

Setting (§2.1, p. 2): i.i.d. data $X=\{x^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^N$, each generated by drawing a latent $z^{(i)}\sim p_{\theta^*}(z)$ then $x^{(i)}\sim p_{\theta^*}(x|z)$. The paper wants a general algorithm that works when

1. the marginal $p_\theta(x)=\int p_\theta(z)p_\theta(x|z)\,dz$ and the posterior $p_\theta(z|x)$ are intractable (so EM and mean-field VB integrals fail), and
2. the dataset is large, so batch optimization and per-datapoint sampling loops (Monte Carlo EM) are too slow.

Three tasks: ML/MAP estimation of $\theta$; posterior inference of $z$ given $x$ (coding, representation); marginal inference of $x$ (denoising, inpainting, super-resolution) (p. 2).

The naive score-function gradient estimator of $\nabla_\phi E_{q_\phi}[f(z)]$ has very high variance and is "impractical" (p. 3).

## Method

A recognition model $q_\phi(z|x)$ approximates the intractable posterior. It is not necessarily factorial and its parameters are learned jointly with $\theta$, not computed from closed-form expectations (p. 3). It is called a probabilistic encoder; $p_\theta(x|z)$ is the probabilistic decoder.

> [!definition] Variational lower bound ([[ELBO]])
> $$\log p_\theta(x^{(i)}) = D_{KL}\big(q_\phi(z|x^{(i)})\,\|\,p_\theta(z|x^{(i)})\big) + \mathcal{L}(\theta,\phi;x^{(i)})$$
> $$\mathcal{L} = E_{q_\phi(z|x)}\big[-\log q_\phi(z|x) + \log p_\theta(x,z)\big]$$
> $$\;\;= -D_{KL}\big(q_\phi(z|x)\,\|\,p_\theta(z)\big) + E_{q_\phi(z|x)}\big[\log p_\theta(x|z)\big]$$
>
> (source: Kingma Welling AEVB, eqs. 1-3, p. 3)

The KL term in eq. 3 acts as a regularizer pulling $q_\phi$ toward the prior; the second term is an expected negative reconstruction error (p. 4). See [[KL divergence]], [[Variational inference]].

> [!definition] Reparameterization ([[Reparameterization trick]])
> $$\tilde z = g_\phi(\epsilon, x),\qquad \epsilon\sim p(\epsilon)$$
> $$E_{q_\phi(z|x)}[f(z)] = E_{p(\epsilon)}\big[f(g_\phi(\epsilon,x))\big] \simeq \tfrac1L\sum_{l=1}^L f\big(g_\phi(\epsilon^{(l)},x)\big)$$
>
> (source: Kingma Welling AEVB, eqs. 4-5, p. 3; proof via $q_\phi(z|x)\prod dz_i = p(\epsilon)\prod d\epsilon_i$, p. 4)

Gaussian example: $z=\mu+\sigma\epsilon$, $\epsilon\sim N(0,1)$ (p. 5). Three recipes for choosing $g_\phi$ (p. 5):

1. tractable inverse CDF with $\epsilon\sim U(0,I)$ (exponential, Cauchy, logistic, Weibull, ...);
2. location-scale families, $g=\text{location}+\text{scale}\cdot\epsilon$ (Gaussian, Laplace, Student-t, uniform, ...);
3. composition (log-normal, gamma, Dirichlet, beta, chi-squared, F).

If none apply, accurate inverse-CDF approximations exist at roughly PDF cost (p. 5).

> [!algorithm] SGVB estimators and AEVB (minibatch)
> Generic estimator A:
> $$\tilde{\mathcal L}^A=\tfrac1L\sum_l \log p_\theta(x,z^{(l)}) - \log q_\phi(z^{(l)}|x)$$
> Estimator B (analytic KL, lower variance):
> $$\tilde{\mathcal L}^B = -D_{KL}(q_\phi\|p_\theta(z)) + \tfrac1L\sum_l \log p_\theta(x|z^{(l)})$$
> Minibatch of $M$ from $N$ points:
> $$\mathcal L(\theta,\phi;X)\simeq \tfrac NM\sum_{i=1}^M \tilde{\mathcal L}(\theta,\phi;x^{(i)})$$
> Loop: draw minibatch and $\epsilon$; $g\leftarrow\nabla_{\theta,\phi}\tilde{\mathcal L}_M$; update $\theta,\phi$ by SGD or Adagrad; repeat. Experiments use $M=100$, $L=1$.
>
> (source: Kingma Welling AEVB, eqs. 6-8 and Algorithm 1, pp. 3-4)

The fact that $L=1$ suffices when $M$ is large enough is reported on p. 4. Because $q_\phi$ is shared across datapoints and amortizes per-datapoint inference, AEVB needs no per-datapoint iterative loop (p. 2); see [[Amortized VI]].

> [!example] Gaussian VAE instance
> Prior $p_\theta(z)=N(0,I)$; decoder a Gaussian or Bernoulli MLP; encoder $\log q_\phi(z|x^{(i)})=\log N(z;\mu^{(i)},\sigma^{2(i)}I)$ with $\mu,\sigma$ from an MLP; $z^{(i,l)}=\mu^{(i)}+\sigma^{(i)}\odot\epsilon^{(l)}$. The KL is closed-form:
> $$-D_{KL}(q\|p)=\tfrac12\sum_{j=1}^J\big(1+\log\sigma_j^2-\mu_j^2-\sigma_j^2\big)$$
> so the per-datapoint objective is that sum plus $\tfrac1L\sum_l\log p_\theta(x^{(i)}|z^{(i,l)})$.
>
> (source: Kingma Welling AEVB, eqs. 9-10, p. 5; derivation in appendix B, pp. 10-11)

Decoder forms (appendix C, p. 11): Bernoulli MLP $y=f_\sigma(W_2\tanh(W_1z+b_1)+b_2)$ with $\log p(x|z)=\sum_i x_i\log y_i+(1-x_i)\log(1-y_i)$ (eq. 11); Gaussian MLP with $\mu=W_4h+b_4$, $\log\sigma^2=W_5h+b_5$, $h=\tanh(W_3z+b_3)$ (eq. 12). Single hidden layer throughout.

> [!definition] Full VB on the parameters (appendix F, pp. 12-14)
> A hyperprior $p_\alpha(\theta)$ and $q_\phi(\theta)$ are added; $\tilde\theta=h_\phi(\zeta)$, $\zeta\sim p(\zeta)$. Define
> $$f_\phi(x,z,\theta)=N\big(\log p_\theta(x|z)+\log p_\theta(z)-\log q_\phi(z|x)\big)+\log p_\alpha(\theta)-\log q_\phi(\theta)$$
> and $\mathcal L(\phi;X)\simeq\tfrac1L\sum_l f_\phi\big(x^{(l)},g_\phi(\epsilon^{(l)},x^{(l)}),h_\phi(\zeta^{(l)})\big)$ (eqs. 21-22). Algorithm 2 gives the pseudocode. The paper states no experiments for this case ("left to future work", p. 2). This is the global-parameter version that [[Variational dropout (Kingma 2015)]] cites as "appendix F".

## Results

Setup (p. 6-7): MNIST and Frey Face. Encoder and decoder have equal hidden units: 500 for MNIST, 200 for Frey Face (smaller dataset). Weights drawn from $N(0,0.01)$; Adagrad with global stepsize chosen from $\{0.01,0.02,0.1\}$; $M=100$, $L=1$; a small weight-decay term equals a $N(0,I)$ prior on $\theta$, so training is approximate MAP. Frey Face uses Gaussian outputs with means squashed to $(0,1)$ by a sigmoid.

- **Lower bound vs wake-sleep** (Fig. 2, p. 7): for latent sizes $N_z\in\{3,5,10,20,200\}$ (MNIST) and $\{2,5,10,20\}$ (Frey), AEVB "converged considerably faster and reached a better solution in all experiments". Extra latent variables did not cause overfitting, which the authors attribute to the regularizing KL term. Estimator variance was small ($<1$). Cost: about 20-40 min per million training samples on an Intel Xeon at about 40 GFLOPS.
- **Marginal likelihood** (Fig. 3, p. 8): MNIST, 100 hidden units, 3 latent variables (higher-dimensional latents made the estimator unreliable). AEVB compared with wake-sleep and Monte Carlo EM (HMC sampler) for $N_{train}=1000$ and $50000$. MCEM is not online and cannot be applied efficiently to full MNIST. The paper gives curves, not a table; I read no numeric values off them.
- **Visualisation** (Figs. 4-5, p. 10): 2-D latent manifolds for MNIST and Frey Face, and samples for 2, 5, 10, 20-D latents.

Marginal likelihood estimator (appendix D, pp. 11-12): fit a density $q(z)$ to HMC posterior samples, then
$$p_\theta(x)\simeq\Big(\tfrac1L\sum_l \frac{q(z^{(l)})}{p_\theta(z^{(l)})p_\theta(x|z^{(l)})}\Big)^{-1},\quad z^{(l)}\sim p_\theta(z|x)$$
Reliable only for latent dimension below about 5.

## Limitations

- Stated: requires continuous latent variables and a differentiable $q_\phi$/$g_\phi$; wake-sleep still applies to discrete latents (p. 6). Future work lists hierarchical, time-series, global-parameter and supervised extensions (p. 8).
- Stated: the diagonal-Gaussian posterior is "a simplifying choice, and not a limitation of our method" (p. 5, footnote 2).
- Wiki note: experiments are small (one-hidden-layer MLPs, MNIST/Frey, curves only); the full-VB case is derived but not tested. A Gaussian $q_\phi$ cannot match a multimodal posterior; richer posteriors are developed in [[Intro to VAEs (Kingma 2019)]] (§3, IAF, but that chapter is not in this PDF) and via [[Normalizing flows]].

## Connections

- Contemporary with Rezende, Mohamed, Wierstra 2014 (stochastic backpropagation), which the paper says made the same connection independently (p. 6); the other VAE paper in this folder is the later monograph [[Intro to VAEs (Kingma 2019)]].
- The single-sample SGVB with global weights is what [[Variational dropout (Kingma 2015)]] improves on: sharing one noise draw across the minibatch makes datapoint losses correlated, which the [[Local reparameterization trick]] removes.
- Related to the score-function/control-variate estimators of Blei-Jordan-Paisley and Ranganath (pp. 3, 6), which the reparameterization trick avoids for continuous latents.
- Wiki note: the same reparameterized-ELBO machinery underlies amortized posterior networks used for inverse problems ([[Amortized VI]]) and, loosely, the noise-to-sample maps in [[Diffusion models]] and [[Flow matching]]. Neither is claimed by this paper.
- Uses HMC / gradient-based MCMC only as a baseline and as a likelihood evaluator (appendices D, E). Compare [[SGLD]] ([[SGLD (Welling 2011)]]), the other Welling method for scalable Bayesian learning; SGLD samples parameters, AEVB fits a variational family.
