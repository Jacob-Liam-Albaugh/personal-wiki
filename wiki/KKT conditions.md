# KKT conditions

> [!theorem] KKT conditions for an LP (source: BJS full book.pdf, pp. 238-239, eqs. 5.23-5.25)
> For $\min\mathbf c\mathbf x$ s.t. $\mathbf A\mathbf x\ge\mathbf b$, $\mathbf x\ge\mathbf0$, a feasible $\bar{\mathbf x}$ is optimal iff there are $\mathbf w,\mathbf v\ge\mathbf0$ with
> $$\mathbf A\mathbf x\ge\mathbf b,\quad\mathbf x\ge\mathbf0$$
> $$\mathbf w\mathbf A+\mathbf v=\mathbf c$$
> $$\mathbf w(\mathbf A\mathbf x-\mathbf b)=0,\quad\mathbf v\mathbf x=\mathbf0$$

**Intuition**: at an optimum the objective gradient lies in the cone generated by the gradients of the binding constraints. Equivalently, the multipliers $\mathbf w$ are a feasible dual solution that is complementary to $\bar{\mathbf x}$.

## The LP case

Derived from Farkas' lemma (BJS §5.4, eqs. 5.21-5.25, pp. 237-239): for $\min\mathbf c\mathbf x$ s.t. $\mathbf A\mathbf x\ge\mathbf b$, $\mathbf x\ge\mathbf0$, the conditions feasibility, $\mathbf w\mathbf A+\mathbf v=\mathbf c$ with $\mathbf w,\mathbf v\ge\mathbf0$, and complementary slackness $\mathbf w(\mathbf A\mathbf x-\mathbf b)=0$, $\mathbf v\mathbf x=0$ are necessary and sufficient. The full derivation and BJS Example 5.7 are on [[Farkas lemma]]; the perturbed conditions define the central path of [[Path-following interior point methods]].

## Key facts
- Necessity follows from [[Farkas lemma]]: no feasible direction $\mathbf d$ with $\mathbf c\mathbf d<0$ and $\mathbf G\mathbf d\ge\mathbf0$ (binding rows $\mathbf G$) exists, so $\mathbf u\mathbf G=\mathbf c$ with $\mathbf u\ge\mathbf0$ (BJS eqs. 5.21-5.22, p. 238).
- Sufficiency: for any feasible $\hat{\mathbf x}$, $\mathbf c\hat{\mathbf x}-\mathbf c\bar{\mathbf x}=\sum_{i\in I}w_i(\mathbf a^i\hat{\mathbf x}-b_i)+\sum_{j\in J}v_j\mathbf e_j\hat{\mathbf x}\ge0$ (BJS p. 238).
- The three groups are primal feasibility, dual feasibility and complementary slackness, so for LPs they restate [[LP duality]] (BJS p. 239).
- BJS Example 5.7 shows a point failing the test because the multipliers would have to be negative (BJS p. 239).
- The barrier conditions of the [[Interior point method]] are the KKT conditions of the barrier problems with complementarity relaxed to $\mathbf X\mathbf S\mathbf e=\mu\mathbf e$ (BT Lemma 9.5, p. 421), and the Newton step of primal-dual path following is applied to that perturbed system (BJS ch8, §8.6).
- The out-of-kilter optimality test (Theorem 11.1) is complementary slackness for the bounded-variable network LP ([[Network flow problem]]).

## General convex programs (Basu §4.1, §4.3)

For $\inf_{x\in C}f(x)$ with $C$ closed convex and $f$ convex, a local minimiser is global (p. 67). Tangent and normal cones at $x\in C$:
$$T_C(x)=\mathrm{cl}\,\mathrm{cone}(C-x),\qquad N_C(x)=\{s:\langle s,y-x\rangle\le0\ \forall y\in C\}.$$
(Defs 4.4-4.5, Prop 4.6, p. 68; the normal cone is the polar of the tangent cone, see [[Convex set]].)

> [!theorem] Optimality conditions (Basu pp. 68-69)
> $x^*$ minimises $f$ over $C$ iff either of these holds:
> 1. $f'(x^*;r)\ge0$ for all $r\in T_C(x^*)$.
> 2. $0\in\partial f(x^*)+N_C(x^*)$.
>
> Condition 2 needs a constraint qualification (a relative-interior point of both domains). Unconstrained case: $0\in\partial f(x^*)$.

With conic constraints $G(x)\preceq_K0$ the multiplier form is the Lagrangian one: at optimal $x^*,y^*$ with zero duality gap, $\langle y^*,G(x^*)\rangle=0$ (complementary slackness) and $x^*$ minimises the Lagrangian; equivalently $(x^*,y^*)$ is a saddle point (Basu Thms 4.32-4.33, see [[Lagrangian duality]]). Slater's condition guarantees no gap and attained multipliers, so for convex programs the KKT conditions are necessary and sufficient. The subgradient $0\in\partial f(x^*)$ test is what the [[Subgradient method]] drives towards.
