# Seven Sketches ch6 - Electric circuits

**Summary**: Colimits and cospans, Frobenius monoids and hypergraph categories, decorated cospans for circuits, and operads with their algebras.

**Authors**: Brendan Fong, David I. Spivak · **Year**: 2019 · **File**: `DSL/Spivak, Fong - Seven Sketches in Compositionality.pdf`

---
## Problem

Compose circuits by gluing terminals. Gluing is a pushout, so the right setting is cospans with colimits; decorating the apex with circuit data gives a hypergraph category, and operads describe the wiring-diagram patterns more flexibly. (source: Seven Sketches, ch. 6, pp. 183-219)

## Colimits (pp. 185-194)

> [!definition] Initial object, coproduct, pushout (Defs 6.1, 6.11, 6.19; pp. 185-188)
> An initial object has a unique morphism to every object. A coproduct $A + B$ has injections and a unique copairing $[f, g]$ out of it. A pushout $X +_A Y$ of $f : A \to X$, $g : A \to Y$ is the universal cocone over the span. (source: Seven Sketches, pp. 185-188)

- Ex 6.2 (p. 185): initial objects in preorders are bottom elements. Ex 6.14 (p. 186): coproducts in Set and FinSet are disjoint unions; coproducts in a preorder are joins (Exercise 6.13). Remark 6.9 (p. 186): "the" versus "a" colimit.
- Ex 6.22-6.29 (pp. 189-190): pushouts in a preorder, pushouts of isomorphic spans, pushouts in FinSet as a quotient of a disjoint union.
- Def 6.30 (p. 192): finite colimits. Prop 6.32 (p. 192): $C$ has all finite colimits iff it has an initial object and all pushouts. Cor 6.36 (p. 193): Set and FinSet have finite colimits; Thm 6.37 (p. 193) gives the formula (quotient of the coproduct of the $D(v)$ by the relation generated by the arrows). Exs 6.38-6.42 (pp. 193-194): initial, coproduct, coequalizer as special cases.

## Cospans and hypergraph categories (pp. 194-204)

> [!definition] Cospan category (Defs 6.43, 6.45; pp. 194-196)
> A cospan $A \to N \leftarrow B$ has feet $A, B$ and apex $N$. If $C$ has finite colimits, $\mathrm{Cospan}_C$ has the objects of $C$ and equivalence classes of cospans as morphisms, composed by pushout; it is symmetric monoidal with unit the initial object and product the coproduct. (source: Seven Sketches, p. 196)

> [!definition] Frobenius monoid (Def 6.52, p. 198)
> An object $X$ with a commutative monoid $(\mu, \eta)$ and cocommutative comonoid $(\delta, \epsilon)$ satisfying the Frobenius law and the special law ($\mu ; \delta = \mathrm{id}$). (source: Seven Sketches, p. 198)

- Def 6.54 (p. 199): the spider $s_{m,n}$ built from $(m-1)$ multiplications and $(n-1)$ comultiplications. Thm 6.55 (p. 199): a connected diagram built from spiders and symmetries equals the spider of its type.
- Thm 6.58 (p. 200): the free prop on the four generators with the nine Frobenius equations is equivalent to $\mathrm{Cospan}_{\mathsf{FinSet}}$.
- Def 6.60 (p. 201): a hypergraph category is a symmetric monoidal category with a Frobenius structure on each object, compatible with $\otimes$. Ex 6.61 (p. 202): $\mathrm{Cospan}_C$ is one. Ex 6.64 (p. 202): $\mathsf{Corel}$. Prop 6.66 (p. 203): hypergraph categories are self-dual compact closed.

## Decorated cospans (pp. 204-212)

> [!definition] Decorated cospan (Def 6.75, p. 206)
> For $(F, \phi) : (C, +) \to (\mathsf{Set}, \times)$ symmetric monoidal, an $F$-decorated cospan is a cospan in $C$ with an element $s \in F(N)$ of the apex's decoration set. (source: Seven Sketches, p. 206)

- Thm 6.77 (p. 207): $\mathrm{Cospan}_F$ is a hypergraph category. Ex 6.69 (p. 204): the power-set functor is monoidal-like and gives sets of decorations; circuits of resistors and similar are the running example (Def 6.83 and Eq. 6.83, p. 209).

## Operads (pp. 213-219)

- Examples of operads (pp. 214-215): context-free grammars versus operads (Ex 6.92), the operad Set of sets and functions (Ex 6.93), the operad Cospan (Ex 6.94).
- Def 6.97 (p. 216): the operad $O_C$ underlying a symmetric monoidal category: types are objects, operations $C_1 \otimes \cdots \otimes C_n \to D$ are morphisms, substitution is $f \circ_i g$.
- Def 6.99 (p. 217): an algebra for an operad $O$ is an operad functor $O \to \mathsf{Set}$, filling boxes. Ex 6.100 (p. 217): circuits with finite-set types.
- Prop 6.101 (p. 218): Cospan-algebras are equivalent to hypergraph props.
