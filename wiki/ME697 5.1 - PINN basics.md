# ME697 5.1 - PINN basics

**Summary**: A [[PINN]] puts the physics in the loss as a regularizer. The notebooks solve a steady heat equation and show three practical points: enforce boundary conditions by construction, non-dimensionalize, and fight spectral bias with Fourier features. An energy-functional loss is the alternative to the residual loss.
**File**: `advanced-scientific-machine-learning/book/pinns/basics/` (`forward.ipynb`, `spectral_bias.ipynb`, `energy.ipynb`)

---

## Topics
- Module takeaway: PINNs encode the physics through a regularization term added to the loss (source: pinns/intro.md).
- Toy problem: steady heat equation, zero Dirichlet data.
- Hard boundary conditions by multiplying the network with a function that vanishes on the boundary.
- [[Non-dimensionalization]] so that a Glorot-initialized MLP sees inputs and outputs of order 1 ([[Weight initialization]]).
- [[Spectral bias]] and random [[Fourier features]].
- [[Energy functional]] loss versus residual loss.
- Follows Wang et al. 2023 (arXiv:2308.08468) (source: forward.ipynb).

## Key results

> [!definition] Toy problem (forward.ipynb)
> Find $u$ on $[0,L_x]\times[0,L_y]$ with $u=0$ on the boundary:

$$
-k\,\Delta u = f.
$$

The source $f$ is derived with sympy from a chosen exact solution:

$$
u = u_0\sin^2(\pi x/L_x)\sin^2(\pi y/L_y).
$$

Values: $u_0=500$ K, $k=10$, $L_x=0.1$, $L_y=1.0$.

**Boundary conditions by construction.** Use the ansatz

$$
u(x,y) = x(1-x)\,y(1-y)\,\mathrm{MLP}(x,y;\theta).
$$

**Residual loss as an expectation.** With $X$ uniform on $\Omega$ (density $1/|\Omega|$):

$$
\mathcal L(\theta) = \int_\Omega [\Delta u + f]^2\,dx
= |\Omega|\;\mathbb E_X\big[(\Delta u + f)^2\big].
$$

Each step averages over 256 fresh random collocation points, so the gradient is unbiased. Optimizer: [[Adam]] at $10^{-3}$.

**Recommended architecture** (source: forward.ipynb): tanh activation, width 128-512, depth 3-5, Glorot init.

> [!failure] Unscaled problem does not train
> With the physical $L_x, L_y, u_0$ the loss does not decrease. Glorot assumes inputs and outputs of order 1.

**Fix: non-dimensionalize.** Set $\tilde x=x/L_x$, $\tilde y=y/L_y$, $\tilde u=u/u_s$, $\tilde f=f/f_s$. The scaled PDE has coefficients

$$
\tilde k_x = \frac{k\,u_s}{L_x^2 f_s},
\qquad
\tilde k_y = \frac{k\,u_s}{L_y^2 f_s}.
$$

Choice: $f_s=\|f\|_\infty$ and $u_s$ from $f_s$, $k$, the larger of $1/L_x^2, 1/L_y^2$ and the size of $\partial^2\tilde u$. The book says not to take the equality "too seriously" (source: forward.ipynb). Printed values: $f_s=9.96\times10^6$, $u_s=4.40\times10^4$, $\tilde k_x=4.415$, $\tilde k_y=4.415\times10^{-2}$. The scaled model trains in 2000 iterations and reaches a good solution. "Very hard to achieve better error with plain MLPs."

> [!note] Not for forward problems
> The book states PINNs are not recommended for forward problems; classical solvers are more efficient (source: forward.ipynb). Their payoff is parametric and inverse problems, see [[ME697 5.2 - Parametric PINNs]] and [[ME697 5.3 - PINNs for inverse problems]].

**Spectral bias** (source: spectral_bias.ipynb). Networks learn low frequencies first (Rahaman et al. 2019; explained by the [[Neural tangent kernel]]). Demo target:

$$
f(x) = \sin(2\pi x) + 0.5\sin(16\pi x).
$$

Setup: 1000 points, noise 0.1, MLP width 128, depth 4, tanh, Adam, 300 epochs. The high-frequency part appears late.

**Remedy: random Fourier features** (Tancik et al. 2020). Fix $B\in\mathbb R^{m\times d}$ with entries $N(0,\sigma^2)$ and feed

$$
\phi(x) = [\cos(Bx);\ \sin(Bx)].
$$

$B$ is frozen (Equinox `eqx.partition` with a filter spec). Wang et al. suggest $\sigma\in[1,10]$ for PINNs. The PINN run uses 100 features and $\sigma=6$.

**Energy loss** (source: energy.ipynb), details in [[Energy functional]]:

$$
E[u] = \int \big[\tfrac k2 |\nabla u|^2 - f u\big]\,dx.
$$

Only first derivatives appear, so the trial space is $W^{1,2}$ rather than $W^{2,2}$. The book finds it "actually not as accurate as the residual loss" in its setup, probably needing more iterations (5000 here, Fourier MLP width 256).
