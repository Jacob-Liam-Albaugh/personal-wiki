# Farkas lemma

> [!theorem] Theorem 4.6, Farkas' lemma (p. 165)
> Exactly one holds: (a) there is $\mathbf x\ge\mathbf0$ with $\mathbf A\mathbf x=\mathbf b$; (b) there is $\mathbf p$ with $\mathbf p'\mathbf A\ge\mathbf0'$ and $\mathbf p'\mathbf b<0$.

> [!theorem] Lemma 5.1, Farkas' Lemma (p. 234, image seen)
> Exactly one of the systems has a solution:
> $$\text{System 1: }\ \mathbf A\mathbf x\ge\mathbf0,\ \ \mathbf c\mathbf x<0$$
> $$\text{System 2: }\ \mathbf w\mathbf A=\mathbf c,\ \ \mathbf w\ge\mathbf0$$

**Intuition**: either $\mathbf b$ lies in the cone generated by the columns of $\mathbf A$, or a hyperplane separates it from that cone. A vector in (b) is a certificate of infeasibility. In BJS form System 2 says $\mathbf c$ is in the cone generated by the rows of $\mathbf A$ (BJS p. 236-237). With $\mathbf y=-\mathbf x$, System 1 has a solution iff the cone $\{\mathbf y:\mathbf A\mathbf y\le\mathbf0\}$ meets the open half-space $\{\mathbf y:\mathbf c\mathbf y>0\}$ (BJS Figures 5.2-5.3).

## Proofs

- From [[LP duality]] (BT p. 165): if (b) held with a feasible $\mathbf x$, then $\mathbf p'\mathbf b=\mathbf p'\mathbf A\mathbf x\ge0$, a contradiction. If (a) fails, the pair "maximize $\mathbf 0'\mathbf x$ s.t. $\mathbf A\mathbf x=\mathbf b,\mathbf x\ge\mathbf0$" and "minimize $\mathbf p'\mathbf b$ s.t. $\mathbf p'\mathbf A\ge\mathbf0'$" has an infeasible first member and a feasible second ($\mathbf p=\mathbf0$), so the second is unbounded and yields the certificate.
- From the [[Simplex method]] (BJS §5.3, p. 234): constructive proof; the lemma is the tool used to establish the KKT conditions below. BJS also state an alternative form as Corollary 5.1 (headings only).
- From separating hyperplanes (BT §4.7, p. 169-172). Closedness of polyhedra is proved first, then Weierstrass' theorem (Thm 4.10, p. 170: a continuous function on a nonempty closed bounded set attains its extremes).

> [!theorem] Theorem 4.11, separating hyperplane (p. 170)
> If $S\subset\mathbb R^n$ is nonempty, closed and convex and $\mathbf x^*\notin S$, there is $\mathbf c$ with $\mathbf c'\mathbf x^*<\mathbf c'\mathbf x$ for all $\mathbf x\in S$.

The proof minimizes the Euclidean distance to $\mathbf x^*$ over $S$ intersected with a ball. Applied to the cone generated by the columns of $\mathbf A$, this re-derives Farkas' lemma and then strong duality without the simplex method (see [[Convex set]]).

## Variants

Corollary 4.3 and Theorem 4.7 (BT p. 166-167) give the same alternative for inequality systems $\mathbf A\mathbf x\le\mathbf b$. Theorem 4.8 (p. 168) reads the result as absence of arbitrage in a one-period market. The cone machinery behind these results (cones, extreme rays, resolution theorem, BT §4.8-4.9) is in [[Polyhedron]].

## Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions for an LP

Consider $\min\mathbf c\mathbf x$ s.t. $\mathbf A\mathbf x\ge\mathbf b$, $\mathbf x\ge\mathbf0$ (BJS §5.4, p. 237-245). At a feasible $\bar{\mathbf x}$ let $I=\{i:\mathbf a^i\bar{\mathbf x}=b_i\}$ and $J=\{j:\bar x_j=0\}$ be the binding constraints. No improving feasible direction $\mathbf d$ (with $\mathbf c\mathbf d<0$, $\mathbf G\mathbf d\ge\mathbf0$ for the binding rows $\mathbf G$) can exist at an optimum, so Farkas' lemma gives multipliers with $\mathbf u\mathbf G=\mathbf c$, $\mathbf u\ge\mathbf0$, that is (eq. 5.21-5.22, p. 238):
$$\sum_{i\in I}w_i\mathbf a^i+\sum_{j\in J}v_j\mathbf e_j=\mathbf c$$
$$w_i\ge0\ (i\in I),\qquad v_j\ge0\
(j\in J)$$
Geometrically the objective gradient lies in the cone generated by the gradients of the binding constraints. Conversely, if these hold at a feasible $\bar{\mathbf x}$ then for any feasible $\hat{\mathbf x}$
$$\mathbf c\hat{\mathbf x}-\mathbf c\bar{\mathbf x}=\sum_{i\in I}w_i(\mathbf a^i\hat{\mathbf x}-b_i)+\sum_{j\in J}v_j\mathbf e_j\hat{\mathbf x}\ge0$$
so $\bar{\mathbf x}$ is optimal (p. 238). The conditions are thus necessary and sufficient. In the usual form, with $\mathbf w\ge\mathbf0$, $\mathbf v\ge\mathbf0$ (eq. 5.23-5.25, p. 238-239):
$$\mathbf A\mathbf x\ge\mathbf b,\qquad\mathbf x\ge\mathbf0\qquad\text{(primal feasibility)}$$
$$\mathbf w\mathbf A+\mathbf v=\mathbf c,\qquad\mathbf w\ge\mathbf0,\ \mathbf v\ge\mathbf0\qquad\text{(dual feasibility)}$$
$$\mathbf w(\mathbf A\mathbf x-\mathbf b)=0,\qquad\mathbf v\mathbf x=\mathbf0\qquad\text{(complementary slackness)}$$
The vectors $\mathbf w,\mathbf v$ are the Lagrangian multipliers (dual variables), and dual feasibility is feasibility of the dual problem of [[LP duality]] (p. 239). The multipliers $\mathbf w=\mathbf c_B\mathbf B^{-1}$ of the [[Revised simplex method]] are exactly the dual solution that complementary slackness pairs with the primal basic solution.

BJS Example 5.7 (p. 239): minimize $-x_1-3x_2$ s.t. $x_1-2x_2\ge-4$, $-x_1-x_2\ge-4$, $\mathbf x\ge\mathbf0$. A claimed optimum at $(0,0)$ has no binding rows from $\mathbf A$, so $\mathbf w=\mathbf0$, which with $\mathbf v\ge\mathbf0$ cannot give $\mathbf v=\mathbf c=(-1,-3)$. The point is therefore not optimal (the true optimum is the vertex $(4/3,8/3)$, BJS Figure 5.4). The extension to general equalities and bounds (p. 240-245) was not read. Nonlinear KKT is in [[KKT conditions]].

## Convex-analysis proof and the valid-inequality form

Basu states the lemma as: either $b=Ax$ with $x\ge0$, or there is $u$ with $A^\top u\le0$, $\langle u,b\rangle>0$ (Basu Thm 2.25, pp. 14-15). The proof separates $b$ from the cone generated by the columns of $A$, which is closed because finitely generated cones are closed (Lemma 2.26).

Valid-inequality version (Basu Thm 2.85): $\langle c,x\rangle\le\delta$ is valid for a nonempty $\{Ax\le b\}$ iff $c^\top=y^\top A$ and $y^\top b\le\delta$ for some $y\ge0$. An empty system has the certificate $0\le-1$ (Thm 2.88).

## Key facts
- Exactly one of the two systems is solvable; the infeasibility certificate $\mathbf p$ is a dual ray.
- BT and BJS state the same alternative in different dress: columns cone for $\mathbf b$ (BT), rows cone for $\mathbf c$ (BJS).
- Farkas, duality and separation are equivalent routes to the same result: simplex gives Farkas gives KKT; separation gives Farkas gives strong duality.
- Used to derive the [[KKT conditions]]: no improving feasible direction at an optimum means the objective gradient lies in the cone of binding-constraint gradients.
- Attributed to Farkas (1894) and Minkowski (1896) (BT notes, p. 199).
